Water Droplets Simulation

setup python and c++ environments for water droplet simulation. a python file and notebook are setup to develop and test water droplet dynamics on a mesh. currently, focused on modelling position and velocity models in the paper by Zhang et al. 2012. which includes surface tension, viscocity dampening local and global volume correction, water to surface friction and hydrophobicity behaviour as modeled by correcting the boundary angles using additional forces. zhang et al. used a numerical simulation method on a point lumped mass surface representation, with explicit Euler integration. While this worked well in simulating fluid-like behaviour, the current version struggles to maintain a round droplet shape, but rather collapses into a flat puddle. testing shows that this may be due to low surface tension coefficient (gamma), high lumped mass density / gravity, large number of verticies (also contributing to increased mass). However, increasing them led to numerically unstable or underdamped behaviour, where restoring forces from the surface tension accumulates oscilations that breaks the droplet. One can increase the viscosity beyond the recommended range in the paper (0.3~0.5) to dampen the behaviour, which is not ideal as it prevents up from fully reproducing the paper's simulation results. to verify water tension term, we ran the simulation of a isolated mesh in zero-gravity. it was shown that the verticies self-distributes evenly along the surface, as expected. to further test this, we initialised the droplet as a rectangular mesh, showing that the mesh also collapses correctly into a spherical shape. to verify the surface contact angle, we tested the droplet in a gravity environment with different contact angle ranges. we did see a notible effect on the roundless of the droplet, consistent with the literature. although unfortunatley, perhaps due to the low resolution or the lack of vertices or paramter tuning, we were not able to decern a clear droplet to surface boundary as exactly shown in the paper. Another direction is the numerical integration methods. Euler's method suffers with larger step sizes, while from HW4, it is mentioned that Verlet method was more suitable for simulating particles.

We have implemented a water droplet simulation in C++ with OpenGL 3.3. The physics simulations runs end-to-end each frame via SimulationSystem, stepping each droplet through SingleDropletSolver with configurable substeps and explicit Euler integration. The solver applies five sequentially-ordered operators per substep:

  • cotangent-weighted
  • Laplacian viscosity
  • flow for surface tension with volume-stiffness correction
  • contact-line forces enforcing advancing and receding contact angle constraints
  • external force field sampling

Surface collisions are modeled with pushout, bounce, and sliding friction. Volume preservation is tracked per-frame using signed tetrahedral summation over the open mesh, closed by a fan-triangulated boundary cap.

Surface Tention

Surface backends are implemented in PlaneSurface (flat plane with analytically exact closest-point and raycast) and HeightFieldSurface (bilinearly interpolated height grid with finite-difference gradient normals and sphere-marching raycast), both satisfying the ISurface interface used throughout the solver and interaction pipeline. Droplet topologychanges are implemented in MergeSplitController, which detects proximity-based merging and elongation-based splitting each frame, with DropletFactory handling volume-weighted blending for merge and principal-axis offset for split. In each substep, we recompute per-droplet derived quantities (vertex normals, center of mass, average velocity, footprint radius, elongation ratio, and principal axis).

GPU Rendering

DropletGpuCache syncs VAO/VBO/EBO buffers against the live droplet list each frame, and the scene renders with Phong shading via two GLSL programs. Mouse drag interaction projects through Picker raycasting against the active surface into a radial falloff DragForceField. ExperimentLogger records per-frame sim time, render time, and volume error to CSV. We are working on the UiController (Dear ImGui sliders for solver, render, material, and merge/split parameters) and the RefractiveRenderer three-pass pipeline (scene color/depth, droplet G-buffer, full-screen refraction composite) before the full feature set is complete.
